# MELSAN Delivery Workflows — the operating method for the 49-day campaign

**MELSAN =** *Melancholic + Sanguine* temperaments, blended. Melancholic brings depth, structure and exacting standards; Sanguine brings warmth, momentum and visible celebration of progress. The campaign fails if either axis is under-weight: Melancholic failure modes are eligibility misses, factual drift, and silent GenAI-rule violations; Sanguine failure modes are morale collapse, loss of visible progress, and the quiet death of a 49-day plan.

Three workflows govern each day. Reference them by name — *MELSAN-Launch*, *MELSAN-Execute*, *MELSAN-Close*.

---

## MELSAN-Launch (morning, ≈ 20 min)

**Purpose:** convert the four acceptance tests on today's row into a committed, un-ambiguous intention.

| Minutes | Posture | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 5 | Melancholic | Read the four acceptance tests on today's `day-packs/<date>/README.md` aloud. Note any ambiguity in writing. |
| 10 | Melancholic | Stage sources: open the specific URL/book/app page each acceptance test depends on; open the prior-day error log; open the fact-check targets. |
| 5 | Sanguine | Name today's one *flagship win* — a single sentence written at the top of your notebook that says what, if you shipped only one thing today, would make the day a victory. |

**Done when:** four acceptance tests read aloud; sources open; flagship win written in the notebook.

---

## MELSAN-Execute (per block, 45 / 60 / 90 min)

**Purpose:** keep attention on the acceptance test, not on the subject matter in general.

- Phone silenced. One tab per task. Browser pane open to the artefact you are producing.
- Pomodoro inside the block: **25 on / 5 off**. Three cycles for a 90-min block; two for 60; one plus a short tail for 45.
- At the halfway mark of the block, 60-second self-check: *am I hitting the acceptance test or drifting?* If drifting, cut scope back to the acceptance test and finish inside the block.
- Save the artefact to `~/Documents/imperial-york-phd/day-packs/<date>/` *as it is produced*. No "I will write it up later." The write-up is the artefact.
- At block close, write one sentence on paper: *"In this block I shipped __________."* If you cannot write it, you did not ship.

**Done when:** artefact file saved; one-sentence log line written; status box ticked on today's README.

---

## MELSAN-Close (evening, ≈ 25 min)

**Purpose:** make today's work *felt as completed* and queue tomorrow before memory degrades.

| Minutes | Posture | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 10 | Sanguine | For each of the four blocks, one sentence: *what shipped?* Four sentences, read aloud or written. If a block did not ship, name that honestly. |
| 10 | Melancholic | Error log: what missed, what pattern it belonged to (IELTS error class / DSA edge case / SD trade-off ambiguity / PhD source-verification gap), what will be repeated tomorrow until it disappears. |
| 5 | Melancholic + Sanguine | Open tomorrow's row in the workbook's Daily One-Look. Write tomorrow's four acceptance tests and tomorrow's flagship win in your notebook. Close everything. |

**Done when:** today's four-sentence ship log written; error log appended; tomorrow's four acceptance tests + flagship win written in the notebook.

## My own output pattern (how I deliver content for the campaign)

Every artefact I produce for the 49-day plan follows the same four-beat structure:

1. **Sanguine lift (1 line, no padding).** Names what today's win is or what the artefact unlocks.
2. **Melancholic body.** Acceptance test echoed verbatim first; then the artefact that answers it, with sources cited inline; then a rubric self-check so you can grade before you move on.
3. **Honest flags.** Anything unverified, assumed, or outside my scope — called out plainly. Imperial statement text is always outside my scope.
4. **Sanguine send-off (1 line).** The next step, with the exact file to open or action to take.

This is not emoji-driven cheerleading or filler affirmations. The warmth is practical — it makes the next action obvious. The rigour is non-negotiable — it protects against the eligibility and compliance risks already named in the workbook's Risk Register.

---

## Compliance callout (re-read on each statement day)

Imperial's PhD guidelines are explicit: the personal statement (1–2 pages) and the research statement (3–4 pages) must be **written by the applicant without GenAI help**. The MELSAN workflows here, and anything I produce in the day-packs, are *planning scaffolding* — requirement checklists, source lists, outline structures, methodology prompts, mock-defence banks, SD diagrams, DSA solutions, PhD fit notes. Imperial statement text must come from your own keystrokes. The two Imperial statement blocks on Days 32 (6 Nov) and 46 (28 Nov) are explicit about this; apply the rule from Day 1.
